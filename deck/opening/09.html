Potential benefits need a contribution model that works.

0:50 · Oscar · Opening 9/11

The expected benefits are broader than code reuse. [Step 1] Reuse decisions, so every team does not redesign the same integrations. Spread learning, so a reviewed improvement can become available to other adopters. Create more familiar ground for people moving and collaborating across teams. These are potential benefits, not measured savings. [Step 2] They depend on a working agreement. What belongs in the base and what stays local? Who maintains it, and how do consuming companies become contributors? How do we publish and adopt upgrades safely? A repository alone cannot resolve those questions. The model needs ownership, extension boundaries and evidence for change. That is where collaboration becomes a governance problem, and where GitOps gives us a bridge to operations.
